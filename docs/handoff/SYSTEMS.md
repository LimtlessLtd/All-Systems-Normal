## Simulation catalogue

**World and infrastructure**

- Rooms/corridors/doors/fixtures, deterministic strategic A* routing and collision-safe local waypoint movement around fixtures/walls/doors.
- Standard modules (owner idea #78): fixture geometry is a percentage of its room, so `StandardModuleFootprints` gives the device-backed modules installed in most rooms (lighting, climate control, ventilation, door local controls) one canonical footprint in station map units. `FacilitySeeder.NormalizeFixtureLayout` places them first among wall equipment at exactly that size, converted per room and per bulkhead orientation, and never shrinks them like other fixtures. It puts their interaction point on the open floor in front of them. When every bulkhead is lined (a few cramped hydroponics bays), the module keeps its size and is stacked as close to a wall as it fits. Rotated modules are identical in map units; on screen they differ slightly because the map canvas is 8:7. Other fixture families still use the older percentage sizing.
- Door lock/open/manual override/bypass/damage/repair/weld/barricade counterplay. Ordinary crew open traversable closed/unlocked hatches automatically and they auto-close after traffic; role/skill rules gate lock/unlock.
- Physical electrical/mechanical infrastructure: reactor/generator output, distribution bus efficiency, capacitor buffering, machine loads, load shedding, powered door actuators, coolant pumps, O₂ generation, CO₂ scrubbing, water recycling and control-network camera reachability. Life support and environmental propagation depend on the actual powered utility chain. `RestoreSystem` (crew, robots, Ollama) only covers what local controls can undo (`CrewCounterplaySystem.HasRestorableProblem`). For life support (`HasSwitchedOffLifeSupport`), that means a switched-off request or a disabled, not failed, core/O₂/scrubber unit. For a room, it means power/lights/camera/climate/ventilation switched off, but not while the grid has shed that room. A failed unit is maintenance work, and a shed room comes back by itself once generation recovers, usually after someone services a worn reactor or generator. When the grid is shedding, cognition gets a `STATION GRID:` line with the shed rooms and power-source condition. Before this, crew "restored" outages the next upkeep tick undid, and their urgency-88/90 intents blocked maintenance. A soak seed with a 22%-condition reactor lost 8 of 11 crew to suffocation (regression test `RestorableOutageTests`). Load shedding (`StationUpkeepSystem.ShedLoad`) drops rooms in `Priority` order: Recreation, Washroom, Storage, Crew Quarters, Kitchen, Hydroponics, Medical, Control Room, other rooms (Airlock, Containment), and Engineering (O₂, scrubbers, life-support core) last. The Reactor, Generator and corridors are never shed. Restoration goes highest priority first. Both shedding and restoring count a room's own load plus the machines that go dark with it (`SheddableLoad`), so the grid neither over-sheds nor re-sheds a room it just restored.
- Deterministic equipment wear/repair plus rare seeded fault events; qualified crew physically travel to and service degraded machinery.
- Generic physical tampering has its first motive-neutral affordance: `DisconnectDevice`. Ollama cognition may choose an enabled non-door machine physically present in the NPC's current compartment for any reason; `IntentExecutionSystem` + `LocalMovementSystem` make the actor walk to the machine's real fixture interaction point, and only `ActionResolver` can then set the authoritative device disabled. C# records the physical act, never a "sabotage" motive; further cut/loosen/overload/spill/jam/block methods remain backlog slices.
- Machinery noise (owner idea #65, `StationNoiseSystem`): running mechanical devices have a base loudness by type (reactor 30, generator 26, coolant pump 22, life support 18, recycler/O₂ 16, scrubber 14, air handler 10, climate/galley 8; lights, cameras, doors, buses and network are silent). Below `DegradedAt` a unit gets louder with wear, up to 4× near zero condition. Stopped, failed or unpowered units are silent, using the same running rule as the #82 inert-machinery presentation; reactor and generators run regardless of their own room's feed. A room hears its own machines plus machines in rooms joined by an open hatch at half strength (closed hatches block sound). At `DisturbingAt` (30) and above, sleep/rest recovery in `SimulationEngine` (fatigue and sleep-debt recovery) scales down linearly to 0.3× at 70. A healthy Crew Quarters measures 18. Cognition gets a `NOISE:` room-state line naming the loudest sources and device IDs, and the Room Control panel shows NOISE. Doing something about it (the existing `DisconnectDevice`, closing a hatch, sleeping elsewhere) is left to minds; fallback minds don't react to noise yet.
- Room temperature (`EnvironmentSystem.TickTemperature`): a powered room with online climate control moves to its setpoint. Corridors and airlocks have no local climate control. While the central air loop reaches them (life support online, room powered, ventilation on), they settle at a 1:3 blend of hull heat loss (11C) and the loop (21C), about 18.5C. Without the loop, heat loss wins and they cool toward 11C. Crew take "cold room" stress below 16C and "hot room" stress above 28C. Before this, heat loss and the loop were applied as two fixed steps, so every corridor slid to 11C after ~18h. Every crew member crossing one took cold stress all day, and whole crews passed the stress ceiling for fighting fires (`ShouldFightFire` requires stress < 88).
- Physical airlocks, pressure cycling and decompression. A near-vacuum compartment (`EnvironmentSystem`, keyed from authoritative `PressureKpa`, not O₂ concentration) loses its normal powered/passive thermal equilibrium and cools deterministically toward a deep-space-cold floor; restoring pressure lets it recover through the ordinary climate/passive/air-loop branches. An oxygen-depleted but still pressurised compartment is hazardous but is not vacuum and keeps ordinary thermal behaviour. Decompression spreads through every atmospherically open hatch (`EnvironmentSystem.FindVacuumDepths`; depth = hatches crossed from the breach or open exterior hatch). `VacuumConsequenceSystem` sweeps a person out into space (no body aboard) only below 25 kPa and only at depth ≤ `MaxEjectionDepth` (1): the exposed compartment and the room directly behind its open hatch. Deeper crew stay aboard and take `SimulationEngine`'s ordinary sub-70 kPa pressure damage, so they can still flee or be sealed off. If they die, they die "from decompression" and leave a discoverable body. Crew seal a decompression (`DecompressionContainmentRules.FindHatchTowardBreach`): anyone standing in a draining compartment (depth ≥ 1) beside a closable open hatch whose far side is closer to the breach, with nobody mid-crossing it, has that hatch as a grounded opportunity. Both fallback minds take it ahead of flight, even before their room crosses the danger line (`BrowserMindSystem.HandleDecompressionContainment` every tick; the server wakes such crew through its emergency path). Ollama sees a non-prescriptive `DECOMPRESSION:` line and chooses for itself. It is the ordinary one-minute `CloseDoor`, which may interrupt committed work while the room drains (`CrewTaskSystem.CanInterruptForLifeThreat`). Ordinary hatches stay openable from either side, so nobody is trapped. In the 2026-09-24 soak seed (roster 15838, station 202), a fire-driven hull breach used to drain all 29 rooms and kill all 12 crew. The crew now confine it to the breached room, and everyone survives (regression test `DecompressionContainmentTests`). Crew in the room directly beside the opening can still be swept out before the hatch shuts.
- Fire/smoke hazards expose composable crew affordances (fight fire, evacuate, seal, vent) rather than scripted responses. Unattended oxygen-fed fire escalates; oxygen starvation progressively accelerates decay, and effectively oxygen-free compartments extinguish immediately before further heat/smoke/hull damage or spread. Conventional suppression is intentionally limited; severe fire spreads through connected/open compartments, damages hull integrity and can create a real breach. Smoke accumulates and diffuses through open hatches, sealed hatches contain it, dense smoke drives visibility toward zero and causes stress/health damage. Cognition receives live topology, hatch/atmospheric connectivity and per-room fire/smoke/visibility/ventilation so minds can invent evacuation/isolation/venting plans that C# validates.

**Crew life**

- Autonomous crew with skills, traits, relationships, beliefs, memories and persistent intents. Fresh rosters start with deterministic relationship texture: `FacilitySeeder.InitialBond` hashes each unordered name pair into a reproducible slice of rivalries/close bonds (with per-direction jitter) before demo overrides.
- Fresh rosters follow a uniform size contract (owner idea #88, `RosterCompositionRules`): each fresh run draws one of the 36 combinations of 4–12 crew and 1–4 robots with equal probability (`Sample(seed)`; Pages derives it from the roster seed, the server from a fresh random seed). Robots are seeded by `FacilitySeeder.CreateDefault(..., robotCount)`; a scenario's `RequiredRobotCount`/robot rooms stay hard minimums, and omitting the count keeps the historical single robot. A continuing campaign keeps its surviving crew, but each new station still draws its robot count. Both runtimes pass `RosterCompositionRules.ConstraintsFor(scenario, roster.Count)`, a per-run copy (`StationGenerationConstraints.WithPlannedCrewCount`) whose `PlannedCrewCount` is the roster actually aboard (sampled crew + scenario prisoners, or a continuing campaign's survivors). That count sizes the hydroponics room (scale clamped to 0.82–1.38) and the opening HYDROPONICS CAPACITY check. The shared scenario constraints are never mutated. Roster provenance is explicit: Ollama/server generation or deterministic seeded Pages generation (`SeededCrewRosterGenerator`). Ollama may generate the six specialist roles, and `CrewRosterScalingSystem.FitToSize` supplements or trims deterministically to the sampled size. Roles cycle Commander, Engineer, Security, Doctor, Technician, Scientist, so the 4-crew minimum always covers command, engineering, security and medicine. `Prisoner` is scenario-composed, never a generated role. Continuing scenarios reconstruct persisted campaign crew and never silently substitute a new roster.
- Daily routines with day/night shifts, scheduled sleep, sleep debt, fatigue-driven slowdown and cognitive skill penalties. Sleep/rest restores only once the person physically reaches a bed/rest fixture. Off-shift continuity (`ScheduledSleepRules`): inside their sleep window, crew stay in bed through mild hunger (the routine and both fallback minds only get up for critical hunger, a full bladder or an escalated missing-person concern); routine provisioning skips off-shift crew unless the galley has no meals; maintenance only calls them out at `OffShiftCallOutUrgency`; ambient social pairing never starts a conversation with someone physically asleep; and hunger/bladder rise more slowly while physically asleep (`SimulationEngine.SleepingHungerPerMinute`/`SleepingBladderPerMinute`); they also breathe at `EnvironmentSystem.SleepingRespirationFactor` (0.6), so a normally ventilated dorm of sleepers stays below the CO₂ danger line (it used to cross 1.25% within the hour and trigger all-night mass evacuations). Unventilated/sealed rooms still become dangerous. Personal needs can pre-empt routine holds but never a committed `CrewTaskState` unless a real survival emergency exists. Scheduling must not reissue `Work` (or refresh work chatter) for someone already working in their duty room.
- Per-crew stat log (owner idea #91): `Npc.StatLog` is a bounded (`StatLogSystem.Capacity` = 40), newest-first list of `StatLogEntry` (stat, cause, signed delta, start/last time) for Health, Stress, Fear, Hunger and Fatigue. Consequence sites record through `StatLogSystem.Set`, which assigns the value the site already computed, so the log never changes the arithmetic (a seeded soak is bit-identical with and without it). Every simulation write to those five stats goes through it: physiology, where `SimulationEngine`'s combined stress update is split by cause via `RecordParts`, plus fire/smoke, social, medical, turrets, robots, prisoners, vacuum, comms, pacts, missing-person and evidence. `StatLogTests.EverySimulationWriteToACoreStatGoesThroughTheLog` guards against direct assignment. The only exceptions are robot health and the death clamp of an already non-positive Health to 0. A cause acting again within `CoalesceGap` (3 min) extends its entry, so continuous drift is one line. The Inspector's STAT LOG card shows entries ≥0.1, green when good for the person and red when bad. Presentation/diagnostic only: never read by cognition and not persisted in campaign saves.
- Task progress presentation (owner idea #84): while `Npc.ActiveTask.Status == InProgress`, the map crew nameplate carries a thin bar of `CrewTaskState.ProgressPercent`, absolutely positioned to the nameplate's own width (task duration only changes fill speed). Ended tasks drop the map bar; the Inspector keeps the outcome but labels it `COMPLETED/INTERRUPTED/FAILED · NOT ACTIVE`, muted, with "Ended at N%". Presentation-only.
- Upright crew figures (owner idea #86): `Home.razor` `CrewStyle` pins the pose animations' `--crew-facing` to 0deg so the human body never rotates. Facing is shown by mirroring the figure left/right (`--crew-flip` on `.person-orientation`), hiding the visor when the heading is mostly up-screen (`.facing-away`), and a small ground triangle (`.crew-facing-cue`) rotated to the exact authoritative `FacingDegrees` (`--crew-heading`). Robots and turrets still rotate. Presentation-only.
- Inert machinery (owner idea #82): `Home.razor` `MachineFixtureClass` adds `fixture-inert` to any non-grow-bed fixture that is not running: a fixture wired to a device needs `StationDevice.IsOperational` (enabled and not failed); anything except reactor/generator sources also needs `Room.IsPowered` (which load shedding clears). One high-specificity CSS rule stops every animation layer on the fixture and its pseudo-elements and dims its glow; clearing the class on restore resumes the original animations. Presentation-only.
- Selected-unit vision arcs (owner idea #87): selecting a visible crew member or a robot draws `PerceptionSystem.VisionOutline` as an SVG polygon in map space: the human forward 180° cone at lit/dark human range (from the observer's own compartment lighting), or a robot's omnidirectional `SensorRange`. Rays march with the same step and wall/closed-or-secured-door rules as the real sightline check, so the arc shows what that unit can actually see. The selected robot's route was already drawn by `SelectedRoutePoints` whenever it has a `TargetRoomId`. Presentation-only.
- In-character bubble lines (owner idea #85): Ollama decisions may include an optional `Say` line (prompted as ≤12 words in the NPC's own voice, presentation only). `OllamaAiDecisionService` flattens it to one line, strips wrapping quotes and caps it at `MaxBubbleTextLength` (90, with …) into `NpcIntent.BubbleText`; the server `GameSession` shows it instead of `Goal` in the thought/alert bubble. Action, target and consequences still come only from the validated fields. The browser/rule-based fallbacks leave it null and keep their deterministic goal text.
- Scarce shared resources (owner idea #20, slice 1): the default washroom has one physical toilet. `UseToilet` relieves bladder need only at that fixture's interaction point, and the simulation grants capacity to only one simultaneous physical user; merely holding the action while walking/waiting gives no relief. This is deterministic capacity only — queue negotiation, yielding and queue-cutting remain cognition-facing follow-up affordances.
- Personal-space preference foundations are usage-derived, not assigned: `Npc.FixtureUseMinutes` records deterministic minutes physically spent at stable room/type/label fixture identities, and `PersonalSpaceSystem.PreferredBedKey` derives the most-used ordinary bed. `BedUseRules` now provides deterministic capacity for active Sleep: concurrent sleepers in one room are ordered stably onto distinct Bed/MedicalBed fixtures, movement heads each sleeper to their assigned bed, and `SimulationEngine` grants restorative sleep only at that assigned fixture. If sleepers exceed bed count, overflow sleepers get no bed/recovery rather than sharing. Relationship-based co-sleep is deferred until explicit romance state exists (#35), and the UI lying pose remains #96 follow-up. Choice bias and displacement irritation remain future slices.
- Seated eating (owner idea #90, slices 1–2): when a mind has chosen `Eat`, `LocalMovementSystem` heads for `DiningSeatRules.SeatFor`. That is a capacity-1 seat in the room (chairs, plus sofas in the recreation room): whoever already sits in one keeps it, and unseated eaters take the free seats in name/id order. If no seat is free, the eater stands at the table or counter. While eating (prepared or raw), a seated eater gets `SeatedStressReliefPerMinute` (0.06) of relief and a standing eater with no free seat gets `StandingStressPerMinute` (0.04) of stress; walking to a free seat is neutral. Food lives in the galley. An `Eat` intent whose target is a Recreation room or Crew Quarters (`DiningSeatRules.DiningRoomFor`; any other target means the galley) walks to the galley, collects one meal (`CarriedMealSize`, taken from `Stores.Meals` into `Npc.CarriedMealPortion`, with an event-log line), carries it there and eats it; with no prepared meal to take they eat in the galley instead. A carried meal is eaten before galley stock anywhere. Away from the galley it is the only food, and when it runs out the eater goes Idle ("Finished the meal I brought"). `ActionResolver` allows `Eat` in those rooms only while carrying a meal. The hunger routine lets a carrier finish in place rather than walking back. Both fallback minds use the shared `DiningSeatRules.FallbackDiningTarget`: carry the meal to a reachable recreation room with a free seat only when every galley seat is taken and they aren't already seated. Ollama keeps only a Recreation/Crew Quarters `Eat` target (anything else becomes null). Cognition, when eating or hungry, gets a `SEATS:` line in the galley, lounge or quarters, and a `DINING:` line with the galley's meal count, whether they carry a meal, and the free seats in each reachable other dining room. Where to eat stays the mind's choice. The authored galley has four chairs round the mess table. In a 30-seed × 8h soak, 86% of eating minutes were seated and 1% standing, with no take-aways (identical to before slice 2). With the galley cut to one chair, the fallback minds made 15 take-aways (122 lounge eating minutes), with deaths, hunger and food stock unchanged.
- Role succession (owner idea #74, slice 1): `Npc.Role` is settable, and only `ActionKind.AssumeRole` (target: a post name) changes it during a run. `RoleSuccessionRules.CanAssume` opens a post to someone only when all of these hold: nobody alive and aboard holds it; they personally found the body of a holder (`Npc.DiscoveredBodies`); they are not a prisoner; their best relevant skill is at least `MinimumSkill` (40: Doctor uses Medicine/First Aid, Commander Leadership, Engineer Engineering, Security Security, Technician Electrical, Scientist Research). Post holders are treated as the public manifest, but deaths are not. Taking a post is instant. Duty room, shift and role-gated work (a new Doctor treating patients) follow from `Role`. The event log and the person's own memory record it, and same-room witnesses get a `MoralActorName` memory for the #16 witnessed-decisions block. Cognition sees a `VACANT POSTS YOU COULD STEP INTO` block listing only posts it could take, never the floor itself. Both fallback minds use `RoleSuccessionRules.FallbackPostToTake`: take an open post only when someone else still holds their current one. A 12-seed × 12h browser-mind soak (12 crew, every Doctor killed at T+0) had exactly one step-up per run, between T+00:02 and T+00:52, with no role churn and no rejected attempts.
- Recreation activities (owner idea #92, slice 1): the lounge has a wall-mounted `FixtureType.Television` (animated while powered, dimmed by `fixture-inert` otherwise) next to the Game Console. `Recreate` may name an activity from `RecreationActivityRules` as its target, and the name travels on `CurrentAction.TargetId`. Each activity has its own body placement and rates:
  - `watch-tv`: watchers spread over the sofas; 1.45 recreation/min, plus 0.4 social need eased per minute when someone else is watching too; needs power.
  - `play-games`: at the console; 1.8/min; needs power.
  - `read`: the reading chair; 1.1/min and 0.05 stress/min relief (stat-logged); works without power.

  An unnamed `Recreate` keeps the generic 1.45/min. An activity whose fixture is missing or unpowered gives no relief, and the need keeps rising. Both fallback minds and the recreation routine use `RecreationActivityRules.FallbackChoice`: read if the lounge is dark or they are stressed; join people already watching TV if they are lonely; otherwise a stable per-person taste between TV and games. Ollama keeps only a known activity ID. Cognition gets a `RECREATION in <room>:` line listing each activity and whether it is free, already in use by others, or dead for lack of power. Which activity, if any, stays the mind's choice. In a 40-seed × 36h soak, recreation minutes split read 45%, TV 42%, games 13%, and deaths were unchanged against main.
- Hydroponics is physical per grow bay: `Empty → Planting → Seedling → Maturing → ReadyToHarvest → Harvesting → Empty` plus `Dead`. Each bay is independently selectable/controllable; worker navigation resolves the crop-bed identity to its exact fixture label (not fixture-list order). Crew physically plant (short committed task, consumes seed inventory) and harvest. A worker has "arrived" at a bay when `LocalMovementSystem.IsAtInteractionPoint` says so: the same point movement walks them to (the inward point for a wall-side bay), not the authored `InteractionX/Y`. Grow equipment runs at full rate until its condition falls below `DegradedAt`, then slows (floor 20%), the same convention as the generator, bus and coolant (`CrewProvisioningSystem.GrowEquipmentEfficiency`). provisioning completion follows the authoritative `CrewTaskState` timer shown in the Inspector, so a task that reaches 100% cannot remain stuck behind a second drifting completion timer. Disabled planted bays stop growing and die after a deterministic interval; produce appears only at readiness; yield derives from bay capacity; constraints can size capacity for planned crew or restrict crops. Raw-food fallback, food preferences and mood/stress consequences are deterministic.
- Recurring job history (owner idea #18, slice 1): `Npc.CompletedTaskCounts` records successful authoritative provisioning completions by action. Planting/tending, harvesting and cooking increment only after their physical task succeeds; starting, failing or interrupting work does not count. This is history/telemetry only and currently drives no assignment or cognition.
- Memory fades: `MemorySalience` = importance × a half-life that grows with importance (trivia fades in hours, defining moments last ~a day). Prompts use the most salient memories; `MemoryRetentionSystem` caps each crew member at 40 memories every 30 minutes and forgets faded trivia older than a day. Campaign carry-over keeps the most important. `IntentExecutionSystem.FailIntent` records each failed intent as a low-importance memory plus a small stress bump, flagged `Memory.IsFailedAttempt` so it reliably reaches cognition: `NpcPromptBuilder` surfaces the last 3 (within 2 hours) in a dedicated YOUR RECENT FAILED ATTEMPTS block, independent of the general salience-ranked RECENT/IMPORTANT MEMORIES block a fresh 0.35-importance entry could otherwise be crowded out of — without this, a mind could silently retry the exact same rejected action forever, since it might never actually see that it had already failed.
- Secrets (owner idea #14, slice 1): `Memory.IsSensitive` marks a memory as something its holder would reasonably want kept private — currently set on both the hider's own memory and a witness's memory when `ActionResolver.TryHidePossession` succeeds (hiding something is the paradigmatic secretive act). `ConversationTopicSystem.RecentNews` — the pathway that passes along actual memory content, unlike the heat-only `Gossip` topic — never selects a sensitive memory, so C#'s own automatic background chatter can never leak one. `NpcPromptBuilder` surfaces currently-held sensitive memories in their own THINGS YOU KNOW THAT OTHERS WOULD WANT KEPT PRIVATE block, framed as the NPC's own judgment call — the only way a sensitive memory reaches someone else is a deliberate LLM-authored social action naming a specific target. No disclosure trust-penalty consequence and no blackmail-as-pact affordance exist yet; both are separate, later slices.
- Bystanders (owner idea #17, slice 1): co-located crew see visible conflict in PEOPLE HERE ("attacking X" / "arguing with Y", perception-gated), a visible attack on someone else adds a non-prescriptive "A FIGHT IS HAPPENING IN FRONT OF YOU" prompt note, and every attack witness (seen or only heard) gets `NeedsMindReconsideration` so cognition reacts now.
- Witnessed decisions (owner idea #16, slice 1): `Memory.MoralActorName` marks a memory as a record of another crew member's decision — a witnessed attack (`SocialSimulationSystem`), witnessed theft/destruction (`ActionResolver.NotifyPossessionWitnesses`), a witnessed promise kept/broken (`PactCoordinationSystem`), or `StationHazardSystem` venting a compartment while other crew are inside (each perceiving occupant remembers who vented it). `NpcPromptBuilder` lists the latest five under "OTHER PEOPLE'S DECISIONS YOU WITNESSED" for cognition to judge.
- Fear conditioning (owner idea #15): `FearConditioningSystem` runs last among each tick's health-affecting/accounting systems, so it sees an NPC's true end-of-tick Health. Surviving a drop to or below `NearDeathHealthThreshold` (15, deliberately lower than `CrewTaskSystem`'s Health &lt;= 40 "acute injury" threshold) tags one `Memory` with `TraumaRoomId` = the room it happened in, gated by `Npc.NearDeathCrisisRecorded` so an extended crisis produces one memory, not one per tick. `NpcPromptBuilder`'s "PLACES WHERE YOU NEARLY DIED" block lists each such room once, labelled vivid/lingering/faint from the memory's `MemorySalience` score (so dread fades with the memory itself), with a repeat-close-call count and a "you are here now" marker. C# never blocks or biases room entry, and the fallback minds deliberately ignore trauma (they are the model-citizen baseline, per `ARCHITECTURE.md`).
- Collective panic (owner idea #19, slice 1): `PanicAlertSystem` runs once per tick right after cognition decides (whichever mind — Ollama, `RuleBasedAiDecisionService` or `BrowserMindSystem`), so it sees a freshly decided flee-a-dangerous-room intent before movement resolves it. The first tick any present crew member both stands in a `CrewEnvironmentSafety.IsDangerous` room and has an `Intent` of `Move`/`SeekSafety`/`EvacuateHazard`, `Npc.PanicAlertSounded` gates one audible claim per continuous danger episode (resets once the room is no longer dangerous). A co-located crew member gets a `Memory` naming the fleeing person when `PerceptionSystem.CanMakeOut` allows identification, or an anonymous "someone" otherwise; the same shout also carries anonymously through any `Door.IsOpen` hatch into the adjacent room (a closed hatch blocks it), mirroring the open-door convention smoke/sound already follow elsewhere. Every memory is tagged `Memory.PanicClaimRoomId`; `NpcPromptBuilder`'s "PANICKED WARNINGS YOU'VE HEARD" block lists the last 3 within 15 minutes non-prescriptively — reacting immediately, investigating first, or dismissing one as a false alarm is cognition's own call, weighed against the named source's Trust already visible in the RELATIONSHIPS block above it. Nothing here scripts a cascade: only the individual audible claim is built this slice.
- Conversations carry content via `ConversationTopicSystem`: doubts about Overseer, gossip about a third crew member (nudges the listener's view by trust; friends of the subject push back), passing on notable memories (never news about the listener), wellbeing and small talk. Arguments name a cause. Informative talk leaves listener memories and appears in the player LOG. Social rolls include the station seed; pairing order rotates. Retold news (owner idea #4) deterministically degrades instead of copying the source memory verbatim: `Memory.RumourHopCount` counts retellings removed from the original direct witness (0 for anything actually witnessed or deterministically told); `Memory.RumourCoreDescription` keeps the original event text stable across hops so each retelling degrades from the source, not from an already-wrapped string. A first hop (hearing it from whoever witnessed it) keeps the exact original wording; a second hop hedges the language while keeping the content; a third and beyond collapse into a fixed template carrying no real content at all, capped so the chain never nests indefinitely. Cognition sees the degraded text for free — prompts already render a memory's own `Description` verbatim — and comparing a rumour's stated details against physical evidence needs no new pipeline, since the LLM already sees both a memory and the room's deterministic evidence state side by side. Only an event important enough to keep clearing the existing `Importance >= 0.5` newsworthiness bar after each hop's own decay (`Importance * 0.7`, floored at 0.3) can actually chain multiple hops in practice.
- Social clusters are detected, not scripted (owner idea #13): `SocialClusterSystem` recomputes every tick which present crew share a clique, purely as connected components of the existing relationship graph — two crew are friendship-linked only when their `Relationship.Affinity` toward each other is mutually ≥ 65 (the same "close relationship" threshold `Home.razor`'s Inspector and `CrewRoutineSystem`'s pairing logic already use), and cliques transitively chain through shared mutual friends. `Npc.CliqueId` (null while ungrouped) biases propagation, not decisions: `ConversationTopicSystem` gossip between clique-mates moves the listener's view of the subject `CliqueGossipMultiplier` (1.5×) harder, and when two crew argue, each witnessing co-located clique-mate of one party (unless also in the other party's clique) gains `CliqueSidingResentment` toward the opponent plus a memory of it (`SocialSimulationSystem.SideWithCliqueMate`).
- Interpersonal commitments have deterministic durable state via `CrewPact` / `CrewPactSystem`: parties, promise text, creation/settlement time and active/fulfilled/broken status. Cognition decides whether to make, honour or break a promise; C# only validates/records the commitment and applies concrete trust/affinity or resentment/stress/memory consequences when it is settled. Cognition proposes/accepts/settles pacts via the `ProposePact`/`AcceptPact`/`FulfillPact`/`BreakPact` affordances: `PactCoordinationSystem` turns a co-located `ProposePact` into a `PactProposal` on the target (expires after 15 minutes unanswered, mirroring `ShutdownCoordinationSystem`'s invitation shape); `AcceptPact` settles a matching proposal into a real `CrewPactSystem` pact; `FulfillPact`/`BreakPact` let the promisor (only) resolve any of their own active pacts, targeted by pact Id, whenever they choose, settling it via `CrewPactSystem.TryFulfill`/`TryBreak`. All LLM-only for now, like several other social affordances. A settlement is gossip-able: `CrewPactSystem.TrySettle` already leaves both parties a memory, and `PactCoordinationSystem.NotifyWitnesses` gives any other co-located crew member one too, attributed if `PerceptionSystem.CanMakeOut` says they can identify the promisor and unattributed ("someone") otherwise — no separate pipeline needed, since `ConversationTopicSystem`'s existing News-retelling path already passes on any sufficiently important memory that isn't about the listener.
- Personal possessions (owner idea #3, all 5 slices shipped; owner idea #11 slice 1 relaxed Hide/Return from ownership to holder/belief): `state.Possessions: List<PersonalPossession>` gives each crew member 1-2 small personally meaningful items (food stash, photograph, medication, tool, keepsake or weapon) at roster generation, deterministic on name like `InitialBond` (`FacilitySeeder.SeedPersonalPossessions`). Every item starts `CurrentHolderId` = its owner, not hidden, not destroyed. Cognition may `HideItem` anything it currently holds — its own, or something previously borrowed/stolen — via `ActionResolver.TryHidePossession` (gated on `CurrentHolderId == npc.Id`, not ownership), auto-attributing a Locker/Cabinet/SuitLocker/ToolCabinet/Crate/StorageRack fixture; `ReturnItem` (`TryReturnPossession`) retrieves anything currently hidden in the actor's room that the actor's own `KnownPossessions` belief places there — owners included (they know where they hid it because hiding updates their belief, not because they own it), the same rule `StealItem` already used for a hiding spot. Both update the acting NPC's own `KnownPossessions` entry on success, since a non-owner's later `ReturnItem` eligibility depends on their own belief staying current with their own actions. `BorrowItem`/`StealItem` let any crew member take a possession they already know about — `BorrowItem` from a co-located holder willing to lend it (`ActionResolver.TryBorrowPossession` gates on the holder's Trust/Affinity toward the borrower); `StealItem` (`TryStealPossession`) from a co-located holder unconditionally (instant negative trust/resentment for the holder, who is right there) or from a hiding spot the actor *themself* believes is in their current room (no confrontation memory, since the owner isn't present). `DestroyItem` (`TryDestroyPossession`) permanently destroys a possession the actor currently holds (their own, or one previously borrowed/stolen), one a co-located crew member holds, or one hidden in a stash the actor believes is in their current room — destroying it right out of someone's hands costs that person trust/resentment and leaves them a memory, whoever they are, matching `StealItem`'s pattern. Discovery is never omniscient: knowledge is a per-observer belief snapshot, `Npc.KnownPossessions: Dictionary<string, PossessionSighting>` (mirrors `LastSeenCrew`/`CrewSighting`) — holder-or-hidden-location as last actually perceived, not the item's live global state. `PossessionAwarenessSystem` (mirrors `MedicalEvidenceSystem.NoticeBlood`) refreshes a sighting every tick for anyone co-located with a visible holder; `ActionResolver.NotifyPossessionWitnesses` records a sighting for anyone who witnesses a hide/borrow/steal/destroy act itself (same identified-vs-unattributed memory pattern as `PactCoordinationSystem.NotifyWitnesses`, but the sighting itself is granted regardless of identification — the physical fact was still seen even if the actor wasn't). All possession affordances are LLM-only for now, like the pact affordances; `NpcPromptBuilder` exposes YOUR PERSONAL POSSESSIONS ("with you" only while physically holding it; otherwise the owner's own last sighting marked "as far as you know", or "missing; you don't know where it is" once they have found it gone) and OTHER PEOPLE'S POSSESSIONS YOU KNOW ABOUT (rendered from each observer's own belief, which may be stale without them knowing). Stealing or destroying from a hiding spot additionally requires the actor's own belief, not just live state, to place it in their current room. `PossessionTheftNoticeSystem` gives the owner a one-time "missing from where I hid it" memory (importance 0.5, gossip-able, never naming a culprit) for the one case nobody else tells them about — a hidden stash stolen from or destroyed while they were absent — but only once the owner is physically back in the room their belief places it; it then clears their belief. Tracked by `PersonalPossession.OwnerAwareOfCurrentState`, which stays false for a destroyed item so the owner keeps seeing it as missing rather than learning it was destroyed.
- Fire response uses shared deterministic capability rules: `StationHazardSystem.ShouldFightFire` gates whether a crew member can reasonably tackle a compartment fire. Each hazard tick may mark at most one available capable responder per remote fire with `NeedsMindReconsideration` so the event is considered immediately instead of waiting for the ordinary cognition cadence; this does not choose the responder's goal. `FindRemoteFireForResponder` is then available to both fallback minds when they decide. A viable remote fire is considered before ordinary bodily needs and may wake a crew member doing mundane committed work. Responder nomination does not treat an existing high-urgency Eat/Sleep intent as a veto: urgency is only a mind hint, so critical needs are explicitly allowed to reach cognition again and apply the fire-first ladder. Crew already holding a `FightFire` intent are not re-nominated every hazard tick while travelling. Only a cognition-chosen `FightFire` response to a reachable, survivable active fire passes `CrewTaskSystem`'s life-threat interruption gate. Navigation and `IntentExecutionSystem` remain authoritative for travel and task completion.
- Spontaneous social conflict can escalate into deterministic fights from stress, personality, relationships, grievances and circumstances.
- Missing-person logic treats routine separation as normal: active searching starts only after ~12 hours unseen, unless the searcher personally observed danger evidence (the missing person's fresh blood, a recent unsafe airlock tied to their last sighting). Concerned-stage absence does not pre-empt work; shared concern does not instantly interrupt the listener. Knowledge of a concern spreads two ways: passively (`MissingPersonSystem.ShareConcerns`, co-located crew only, gated to a Searching/Escalated concern, a 30-minute cooldown and Trust ≥ 35) or deliberately via the `AskAboutLocation` affordance (any known crew member, no stage/trust/cooldown gate) — cognition picks who to ask, `MissingPersonSystem.ResolveAsk` settles it once co-located: if the askee's own `LastSeenCrew` holds a sighting newer than the asker's concern, the asker's concern/belief updates with an attributed statement ("X told me they last saw..."); otherwise it's a neutral "hadn't seen them" exchange with no belief change. Checking a specific room (workplace, last-known room) toward a concern is already an ordinary `Investigate` of that room — `MissingPersonSystem.UpdateSearchKnowledge` counts presence in the concern's `ExpectedRoomId`/`LastKnownRoomId` as a checked room regardless of how the actor got there.
- Social/check-on intents (`Talk`, `Socialize`, `Argue`, `RequestHelp`, `CheckOnCrew`, `AssistCrew`, `AskAboutLocation`, `CoordinateWork`, `ReassureCrew`, `MisleadCrew`, `ReportConcern`, `ProposePact`, `RecruitShutdownAlly`) path toward each actor's own belief of the target's location — their last direct `LastSeenCrew` sighting, or `CrewDutySchedule.ExpectedDutyRoomId` when they have never crossed paths — never the target's true live room; being physically co-located is real perception and still resolves instantly. Arriving at a stale belief and not finding the target reports the miss (`Idle`) without abandoning the goal: the belief recomputes every tick, so a fresher sighting resumes pursuit automatically, and the goal otherwise expires through the normal intent-lifetime timeout.
- Medical treatment/resurrection is resource/power/body gated; blood evidence persists until a capable actor cleans it.
- `CrewLifecycleAuditSystem` guarantees death/removal transitions are logged and bodies/presence state stay explainable.

**Minds and knowledge**

- **Server Ollama cadence:** ordinary model cognition gets one opportunity every four simulated minutes. `MindCadenceRules.NextIdleMind` scans deterministically from the round-robin cursor for the next crew member without a live intent; a busy crew member never consumes the whole Ollama slot. Emergency/event reconsideration still bypasses this cadence. This scheduler chooses only *who may think now*, never what they want.
- `CrewAffordanceSystem` is the shared capability catalogue for Ollama and browser fallback; C# still validates knowledge, targets, routes, skills, permissions and outcomes. Grounded agency includes cooperative, investigative, deceptive, safety and local door intentions; deception never directly edits another NPC's beliefs.
- Suspicion/evidence, investigation, testimony and account comparison. Broadcasts/private messages are interpreted as claims, not truth.
- Overseer FIRE ALARM (owner idea #89): the toolbar `FIRE ALARM` control arms room targeting; the next room clicked has `OverseerCommsSystem.SoundFireAlarm` send a structured station-wide `OverseerClaimKind.FireAlarm` broadcast naming it. It is graded at send time (false if that room has no active fire; smoke alone does not count), settles like any claim when a listener sees the room, wakes every mind and assigns nobody a goal. Free-text messages cannot produce a `FireAlarm` claim (downgraded to `Warning`). Deterministic fallback minds that heard a credible (`Persuasiveness >= 0.5`) alarm within `StationHazardSystem.FireAlarmResponseWindow` (30 min) may join one existing responder at that fire (two total instead of one); Ollama cognition sees the broadcast in its Overseer-messages prompt block.
- Deterministic perception: human forward-cone/open-door LOS; omnidirectional longer-range machine sensors; hostile assets cannot acquire unseen targets.
- Transient cognition diagnostics via `CognitionTelemetrySystem`: Ollama traces keep the exact request prompt/options, raw provider response and validated intent; browser/rule-based minds emit the same shape. `/debug` gives Ollama traffic a dedicated newest-first I/O panel with the exact request trace and raw response visible side-by-side (rather than buried in collapsed cognition details), and explicitly explains that the static Pages runtime has no Ollama calls because it uses `BrowserMindSystem`.

## Containment transfer (standalone assignment, V0.13)

`containment-transfer` is a complete, player-reachable, deterministic win/lose assignment.

- **Standalone, not part of the campaign.** `ScenarioCatalog.Campaign` stays at 5 entries. `ScenarioCatalog.StandaloneAssignments = [ContainmentTransfer]` holds complete assignments that run on a fresh station/roster and never touch campaign continuity, reveal stage or the endgame gate; `ScenarioCatalog.Find` resolves both. It was kept out of `Campaign` because its `FreshGenerated` roster and dedicated `AsymmetricIndustrial`/Security constraints are incompatible with the continuing-crew model.
- **Entry point:** `StationSession.LoadStandaloneScenarioAsync(scenarioId)` (abstract, implemented on both hosts), wired to a confirm-guarded "SPECIAL ASSIGNMENT: CONTAINMENT TRANSFER" button in the MENU overlay.
- **Escape/recapture/combat** is deterministic in `PrisonerContainmentSystem`. Escape pressure (danger tier + `PrisonerViolenceBias` + stress) creates an attempt only when a real unsecured/passable containment hatch exists. The attempt uses ordinary `ActionResolver`/`NpcMovement`; `LocalMovementSystem` approaches the hatch and revalidates it at the portal, and only a successful crossing sets `Npc.HasEscapedContainment`. `Npc.IsContainmentBreachInProgress` protects the committed crossing from cognition. Locking/welding/barricading the hatch before crossing stops the escape. At large, `CrewRoutineSystem` supplies the farthest-reachable-room fallback. `RecapturePrisoner` is an ordinary mind-chosen crew affordance; C# resolves restraint, injury and lethality. `Attack` is rewritten to `Argue` and is not mind-selectable.
- **Win/lose:** mandatory `DirectiveKind.ContainmentIntegrity` (`CorporateDirectiveSystem`) fails immediately on any prisoner death and is graded at the observation deadline on whether every prisoner is secure. `KeepCrewAlive` and `ScenarioProgressSystem`'s living-crew count exclude prisoners.
- **Presentation:** prisoner tokens/Inspector cards show `BREACH IN PROGRESS`, `AT LARGE` or secure custody; crew pursuing/restraining an escapee show `RECAPTURE`. Presentation-only.
- Coverage: `tests/Overseer.Simulation.Tests/PrisonerTransportTests.cs`.
